Final Expansion – Interactive Learning Experiences & Build Your Own Home

The website is now functionally complete. This final phase is about creating memorable educational experiences that increase engagement while teaching practical housing concepts.

The objective is not to gamify the entire website. Instead, embed optional, bite-sized interactive learning experiences throughout the site that reinforce important housing lessons.

These experiences should feel like hidden discoveries users can choose to engage with as they explore.

Interactive Learning Cards

Rather than using obvious "Play Game" buttons, create Interactive Learning Cards that naturally blend into the page.

They should:

Match the website's overall design language.
Use rounded corners, soft shadows, and subtle gradients.
Feel like premium educational widgets rather than arcade games.
Lift slightly and animate when hovered.
Display a subtle prompt such as:
"Try a 30-second challenge"
"Test your knowledge"
"Interactive scenario"
"Can you make the right decision?"

The interaction should happen inline or in a lightweight modal without taking users away from the page.

Use clean SVG illustrations and soft motion. Avoid the visual style of the main simulator.

Mini-Game 1: Hot Market Challenge

Location: Buying a Home page

Purpose: Teach users that desirable homes can receive offers almost immediately.

Scenario:

A home listing appears as a stylized property card.

Information includes:

Price
Bedrooms
Bathrooms
Neighborhood
Days on market
Estimated monthly payment
A few pros and cons

The player has only 10 seconds to evaluate whether to submit an offer.

Buttons:

Make an Offer
Keep Looking

After the timer ends:

Explain what happened.

Examples:

"Great choice! This home had multiple offers within hours."

or

"This home sold before you made a decision. In competitive markets, preparation matters."

Then provide a short educational takeaway about mortgage pre-approval and being ready to act quickly.

Mini-Game 2: Spot the Hidden Fees

Location: Renting page

Purpose: Teach renters how to carefully review lease agreements.

Present a simplified lease with a mixture of normal and questionable charges.

Examples:

Monthly rent
Security deposit
Utilities
Pet fee
Pest control
Trash service
Duplicate utility charge
Inflated administrative fee
Double billing
Hidden monthly convenience fee

The player must identify the questionable or unexpected charges before signing.

Correct selections explain why users should ask questions before signing a lease.

Mini-Game 3: Home Inspection Decisions

Location: Homeownership Journey

Purpose: Teach users about inspections and protecting themselves during a purchase.

Present realistic situations.

Example:

"The seller recommends using their preferred inspector."

Possible responses:

Accept immediately
Hire an independent licensed inspector
Skip the inspection to save money

Explain the consequences of each choice.

Additional scenarios could include:

Mold discovered after purchase
Roof damage
Foundation issues
HVAC failure
Septic system concerns
Water well testing
Underground oil tank
Electrical problems

Each scenario should include a practical explanation of what a buyer should do.

Educational Value

Every mini-game should conclude with:

"What You Learned"

This should summarize the key lesson in one or two sentences and provide a link to the relevant section of the Housing Hub for additional information.

Remove Floating Town Characters

The decorative SVG townspeople currently behave incorrectly.

As users scroll, they float over content and obscure information.

Remove these floating characters entirely.

If decorative illustrations are retained in the future:

They must be anchored to their section.
They should scroll naturally with the page.
They must never overlap content or interfere with readability.

Redesign it to feel more engaging while remaining appropriate for a government website.

Ideas include:

Interactive category tiles
Soft hover animations
Expandable service previews
Animated icons

Background depth
Better spacing

The goal is to make the section inviting and easy to explore without sacrificing clarity or professionalism.

Accessibility Requirements

All new interactive content must comply with WCAG 2.1 Level AA.

Requirements include:

Full keyboard navigation.
Visible focus indicators.
Screen reader labels for interactive elements.
Sufficient color contrast.
Support for prefers-reduced-motion.
No flashing or strobing effects.
Ability to pause or disable non-essential animations.
All games fully playable without relying solely on a mouse.
Clear instructions before each interaction.
Time-limited challenges should allow users to extend or disable the timer when accessibility settings require it.

The experience should remain fully usable even if animations are disabled.

Overall Vision

The final website should feel less like a collection of pages and more like an interactive digital learning platform. Every interactive element should reinforce practical housing knowledge, reward curiosity, and help first-time renters and homebuyers build confidence. The result should be a polished, accessible, and engaging civic experience that demonstrates how modern government websites can educate, delight, and empower their communities.
